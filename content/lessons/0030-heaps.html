Most urgent first

In real life

A hospital emergency room doesn't see patients in arrival order. The most urgent case is always seen next, even if they arrived last. And the nurse doesn't re-sort the whole waiting room whenever someone new comes in; they just need to know who's most urgent right now.

A priority queue always gives you the item with the highest priority next. The usual way to build one is a heap.{{cite:1}} In a min-heap, the smallest item is always at the top. It's a binary tree (Lesson 28) stored inside a plain list, with one rule: every parent is no bigger than its children.{{cite:2}} That rule is much weaker than "fully sorted", which is why keeping it is cheap.

A max-heap from a min-heap

Python's heapq only does min-heaps. To get the largest first, store negative numbers: the most negative is the smallest, which was originally the largest. Negate again when you take it out.

LeetCode 1046: Last Stone Weight

Each turn, smash the two heaviest stones together. Equal weights destroy both; otherwise the lighter one is destroyed and the heavier one's weight drops by the lighter's weight. Return the weight of the last stone left, or 0 if none are left.{{cite:4}} Use a max-heap of negatives.

Stuck? A hint

While there are at least two stones, pop twice (negating to get the real weights). If they differ, push back the negative of the difference. At the end, return -heap[0] if anything's left, else 0.

The k largest, without sorting everything

In real life

Picking a team of the 3 tallest people in a stadium. You don't need to rank all 50,000 of them. Keep 3 people standing at the front. Each time someone taller than the shortest of your 3 comes along, the shortest sits down and the new person stands up.

To find the k largest items, keep a min-heap of size k. For each number, push it; if the heap grows past k, pop the smallest. At the end, the heap holds the k largest, and its top, heap[0], is the k-th largest. That's O(n log k), better than sorting's O(n log n) when k is small.

LeetCode 215: Kth Largest Element in an Array

Return the k-th largest number (counting duplicates: in [3, 3, 1], the 2nd largest is 3). Use a min-heap of size k, not sorting.{{cite:5}}

Heaps of tuples

Heaps compare items with <, and tuples compare item by item: first elements first, and only if those tie, the second elements. So you can push (priority, thing) pairs, and the heap orders them by priority. This is how you attach data to a priority.

LeetCode 973 asks for the k points closest to (0, 0).{{cite:6}} Distance from the origin is x*x + y*y under a square root, but the square root doesn't change which is closer, so skip it. Push (distance, x, y) tuples.

LeetCode 973: K Closest Points to Origin

Return the k points closest to (0, 0), in any order. Each point is a list [x, y].

Stuck? A hint

Simplest version: build a list of (x*x + y*y, x, y) tuples, heapify it, then pop k times and collect [x, y]. (The size-k heap from the last section is faster when k is small; try it once this passes.)

New words

Priority queue
A queue that hands out the highest-priority item next.
Min-heap
A tree kept in a list where every parent is no bigger than its children, so the smallest is on top.

Python's module of heap functions on plain lists.
Top-k pattern
Keep a heap of size k to find the k largest or smallest items.

1. In a Python heap h, where is the smallest item?

heapq keeps the smallest at index 0, the top of the tree.

2. How do you make heapq give the largest item first?

Negating flips the order, so the largest original value becomes the smallest stored one.

3. What's the cost of heappop on a heap of n items?

Restoring the heap rule moves an item down one path of the tree, about log n levels.

4. To find the 3rd largest of a million numbers, keep a min-heap of what size?

A size-k min-heap holds the k largest so far; its top is the k-th largest.
